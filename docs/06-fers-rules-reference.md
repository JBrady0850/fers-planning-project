## 2. IMMEDIATE RETIREMENT ELIGIBILITY

| Category | Age | Service | Age reduction |
|---|---|---|---|
| Age 62 | 62 | 5 years | None |
| Age 60 | 60 | 20 years | None |
| MRA + 30 | MRA | 30 years | None |
| MRA + 10 | MRA | 10 to 29 years | Reduced |

"Immediate" means the annuity begins no later than 31 days after separation, per 5 CFR 842.204(c)(1)(ii). For a voluntary retirement that is the first day of the month after separation. OPM consumer pages round this to 30 days; the regulation says 31.

Source: OPM FERS Eligibility, https://www.opm.gov/retirement-center/fers-information/eligibility/
Source: OPM Types of Retirement, https://www.opm.gov/retirement-center/fers-information/types-of-retirement/

**The MRA+10 reduction.** Five twelfths of one percent for each full month the retiree is under age 62 at the annuity commencing date, which is five percent per year. Months are counted individually, not rounded to whole years. **The reduction is permanent and does not stop at age 62.**

Source: OPM FERS Computation, https://www.opm.gov/retirement-center/fers-information/computation/
Source: CSRS/FERS Handbook Chapter 42, https://www.opm.gov/retirement-center/publications-forms/csrsfers-handbook/c042.pdf
Source: CSRS/FERS Handbook Chapter 50, https://www.opm.gov/retirement-center/publications-forms/csrsfers-handbook/c050.pdf

**Source conflict, flagged.** The OPM Eligibility page states the reduction loosely as "5 percent a year for each year you are under 62." The Computation page, the MRA+10 FAQ, and Handbook Chapters 42 and 50 all state the monthly rate. The monthly formulation is the operative rule.

**The MRA+20 exception.** A person separating with at least 20 years under MRA+10 who postpones the annuity commencing date to the first day of any month after age 60 takes no age reduction at all.

Source: CSRS/FERS Handbook Chapter 42, https://www.opm.gov/retirement-center/publications-forms/csrsfers-handbook/c042.pdf

## 3. POSTPONED VERSUS DEFERRED RETIREMENT

**Postponed.** Available to someone who separates already eligible for MRA+10. The annuity commencing date may be elected later, up to the second day before age 62, and the age reduction is computed from the commencing date rather than the separation date. FEHB and FEGLI terminate at separation, Temporary Continuation of Coverage is available for up to 18 months at 102 percent of the full premium, and **when the postponed annuity commences, FEHB may be re enrolled and FEGLI reinstated**, provided the five year requirement was met at separation. Filed on Form RI 92-19.

Source: CSRS/FERS Handbook Chapter 42, https://www.opm.gov/retirement-center/publications-forms/csrsfers-handbook/c042.pdf
Corroboration: https://www.myfederalretirement.com/fers-postponed-retirement-mra10-mra20/

**Deferred.** Available to someone who separates with at least 5 years of creditable civilian service having taken no refund. Payable at 62 with 5 years unreduced, or at MRA with 10 years reduced 5 percent per year under 62. OPM states verbatim: **"If you receive a deferred annuity, you are not eligible to reenroll in any health benefits, life insurance coverage, or dental and vision benefits."**

Source: OPM Deferred Retirement, https://www.opm.gov/retirement-center/fers-information/types-of-retirement/tabs/deferred-retirement/

**The decisive distinction.** Only the postponed path preserves the ability to re enroll in FEHB and FEGLI, and the postponed path requires reaching the MRA with at least 10 years **before** separating. Separating before the MRA forces the deferred path and permanently forfeits federal insurance.

## 4. ANNUITY COMPUTATION

```
Basic annuity = multiplier x high-3 average salary x years and months of creditable service
```

**Multiplier: 1.0 percent**, or **1.1 percent** only if, at separation, the employee is **age 62 or older AND has at least 20 years of creditable service**. Both conditions are required, and both are tested at separation rather than at the annuity commencing date.

Source: OPM FERS Computation, https://www.opm.gov/retirement-center/fers-information/computation/
Source: CSRS/FERS Handbook Chapter 50, https://www.opm.gov/retirement-center/publications-forms/csrsfers-handbook/c050.pdf

## 5. HIGH-3 AVERAGE SALARY

**Definition, Handbook Chapter 50, verbatim:** "The largest annual rate resulting from averaging an employee's rates of basic pay in effect over any period of 3 consecutive years of creditable civilian service."

The three years must be consecutive, need not be the final three, and the average is **time weighted**, meaning each rate is weighted by the period it was in effect rather than averaging three annual salary figures.

**Counts as basic pay:** base General Schedule or wage grade rate; **locality based comparability payments**; special salary rates under 5 U.S.C. 5303; standby duty premium under 5 U.S.C. 5545(c)(1) within the 25 percent cap; **law enforcement availability pay and administratively uncontrollable overtime** under 5 U.S.C. 5545(c)(2) within the 25 percent cap; and for wage grade employees, night differential and environmental differential pay.

**Does not count:** bonuses, allowances, overtime, holiday pay, military pay, cash and performance awards, recruitment and relocation incentives, travel reimbursement, per diem, moving expenses, the lump sum payment for annual leave, and any premium pay from which retirement deductions are not withheld.

Source: CSRS/FERS Handbook Chapter 30, https://www.opm.gov/retirement-center/publications-forms/csrsfers-handbook/c030.pdf
Corroboration: https://www.myfederalretirement.com/fers-high-3-salary/

**Point worth stating to the user explicitly.** Locality pay **is** part of basic pay and **does** count toward the high-3. This is frequently misstated. Availability pay and AUO also count where deductions are withheld.

**Not fully verified.** Handbook Chapter 30 affirmatively includes night differential and hazard differential for wage grade employees but does not state the corresponding exclusion for General Schedule night differential and hazardous duty pay in the text retrieved. That exclusion is supported by secondary sources only. Confirm with the agency benefits officer if it is material.

## 6. UNUSED SICK LEAVE

**100 percent credit** toward the annuity computation for FERS retirements on or after January 1, 2014. Fifty percent credit applied from October 28, 2009 through December 31, 2013.

**It does not count toward retirement eligibility and it does not raise the high-3.** OPM states it "cannot be used in computing your high-3 average salary or for meeting the minimum length of service for retirement eligibility."

**It does, however, count toward the 20 years the 1.1 percent multiplier requires.** OPM Benefits Administration Letter 18-103, dated 12 September 2018, states that a retiree who is at least age 62, whose actual service is less than 20 years but increases to at least 20 years with the addition of unused sick leave credit, will have the annuity computed using the 1.1 percent formula. The reasoning is that the 1.1 percent factor is a computation rule rather than an eligibility rule. Most secondary federal retirement commentary still says the opposite.

Conversion basis: **2,087 hours equals one year on the 360 day retirement year, being twelve months of thirty days.** One month is about 174 hours and one day about 5.8 hours. Eight hours to a day is the leave charging rule, not the annuity conversion, and using it overstates the credit by roughly 38 percent. Round **up** to the next figure on OPM's 2,087 hour chart, add the resulting months and days to actual service, and only then drop any leftover days of the combined total. Sick leave days can complete a partial month of real service, so truncating them first loses a month.

Source: OPM RI 83-8, Credit for Unused Sick Leave, https://www.opm.gov/retirement-center/publications-forms/pamphlets/ri83-8.pdf
Source: OPM BAL 18-103, unused sick leave and the 1.1 percent multiplier, https://www.opm.gov/retirement-center/publications-forms/benefits-administration-letters/2018/18-103.pdf
Source: OPM Creditable Service, https://www.opm.gov/retirement-center/fers-information/creditable-service/
Statutory authority: P.L. 111-84, NDAA FY2010, https://www.congress.gov/111/plaws/publ84/PLAW-111publ84.pdf
Implementation: OPM BAL 10-101, https://www.opm.gov/retirement-center/publications-forms/benefits-administration-letters/2010/10-101.pdf

**Stale source warning.** Handbook Chapter 50, dated 1998, still states that unused sick leave is not creditable under FERS. That text predates P.L. 111-84 and is superseded. Do not rely on Chapter 50 for sick leave.

## 7. CREDITABLE SERVICE, DEPOSITS AND REDEPOSITS

**Civilian non deduction service, before January 1, 1989.** A deposit may be made, at **1.3 percent of basic pay for the service plus interest**, compounded annually from the midpoint. **If the deposit is not paid, the service counts for neither eligibility nor computation.** Under FERS there is no annuity reduction alternative as there is under CSRS. A deposit is not considered made for a distinct period unless the total amount due for that period is paid in full, so partial payment buys nothing.

Source: OPM Service Credit, https://www.opm.gov/retirement-center/fers-information/service-credit/
Source: CSRS/FERS Handbook Chapter 21, https://www.opm.gov/retirement-center/publications-forms/csrsfers-handbook/c021.pdf
Source: 5 CFR 842.305, https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-5/chapter-I/subchapter-B/part-842/subpart-C/section-842.305

**Civilian non deduction service, on or after January 1, 1989.** A FERS employee generally cannot make a deposit and the service is not creditable for any purpose. Narrow exceptions exist for Peace Corps, VISTA and certain Foreign Service.

Source: OPM Creditable Service, https://www.opm.gov/retirement-center/fers-information/creditable-service/

**Redeposit of refunded FERS service.** Permitted under P.L. 111-84 section 1904 for employees under FERS on or after October 28, 2009 with a separation on or after that date. The amount is the refund plus interest from the date received, compounded annually. **If the redeposit is not paid, the refunded service counts toward eligibility but cannot be used to compute the annuity.**

Source: OPM BAL 11-103, https://www.opm.gov/retirement-center/publications-forms/benefits-administration-letters/2011/11-103.pdf

**Stale source warning.** Handbook Chapter 21, dated 1998, states that a FERS employee who takes a refund may not make a redeposit. That is superseded by P.L. 111-84 and BAL 11-103.

**Military service deposit.** Pre-1957 military service is creditable with no deposit. Post-1956 military service requires a deposit to count for **either eligibility or computation**. The amount is generally **3 percent of military basic pay**, with variations of 3.25 percent for 1999 service and 3.40 percent for 2000 service. Interest begins on the second anniversary of the FERS coverage start date and compounds annually, with no interest charged if the deposit is completed before the end of the year after interest begins, which yields an interest free window of roughly three years from entry on duty. **The deposit must be paid before separation from federal service. OPM cannot accept it after retirement.**

Source: 5 CFR 842.307, https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-5/chapter-I/subchapter-B/part-842/subpart-C/section-842.307
Source: OPM Service Credit, https://www.opm.gov/retirement-center/fers-information/service-credit/

**Part time proration.** The annuity is computed as if service were full time on the full time high-3, then multiplied by a proration factor equal to actual hours worked during all creditable FERS service divided by total full time hours available in the same period, rounded to the nearest percent. Full time hours are 2,080 per year before March 1, 1986 and 2,087 per year thereafter. Under FERS the factor applies to the entire annuity. Part time service counts as full calendar time for eligibility.

Source: CSRS/FERS Handbook Chapter 55, https://www.opm.gov/retirement-center/publications-forms/csrsfers-handbook/c055.pdf

## 8. THE FERS SPECIAL RETIREMENT SUPPLEMENT

**Eligibility.** Requires an **immediate, unreduced** annuity, at least one full calendar year of FERS service, and an age under 62.

| Retiree type | Eligible |
|---|---|
| MRA + 30 years | Yes, payable at retirement |
| Age 60 with 20 years | Yes, payable at retirement |
| **MRA + 10** | **No** |
| **Deferred** | **No** |
| **Disability** | **No** |
| Early retirement under VERA or discontinued service | Yes, but not payable until the MRA |
| Special provision: LEO, firefighter, ATC, military reserve technician | Yes, payable immediately even before the MRA |

OPM states directly: "If you receive a deferred benefit, a disability benefit or an immediate MRA+10 benefit, you will not be eligible for the annuity supplement."

Source: CSRS/FERS Handbook Chapter 51, https://www.opm.gov/retirement-center/publications-forms/csrsfers-handbook/c051.pdf
Source: OPM Types of Retirement, https://www.opm.gov/retirement-center/fers-information/types-of-retirement/

**Computation, working approximation:**

```
Supplement = (Social Security benefit estimated at age 62) x (years of FERS civilian service / 40)
```

The numerator is total civilian service creditable under FERS, not exceeding 40 years, rounded to the nearest whole number. **Military service does not count in the numerator even where a deposit was paid**, with a narrow exception for active duty covered by military leave from civilian service with a USERRA deposit.

Source: CSRS/FERS Handbook Chapter 51, https://www.opm.gov/retirement-center/publications-forms/csrsfers-handbook/c051.pdf

**Conflict, flagged.** Handbook Chapter 51 states the numerator is rounded to the nearest whole number. Some published guidance rounds up to the next higher year. OPM's text controls; the difference moves the estimate by about one fortieth.

**Start, end, and COLA.**

- Starts at retirement for regular immediate retirees and for special provision retirees, and at the MRA for those who retired earlier under VERA or discontinued service.
- **Ends at the earlier of the last day of the month the retiree reaches age 62, or the last day of the month before entitlement to Social Security begins.** It terminates at 62 whether or not the retiree applies for Social Security.
- **It receives no cost of living adjustment.** Handbook Chapter 51: "The retiree annuity supplement is not increased by cost-of-living adjustments (COLAs)."

**Earnings test.** Earnings above the annual exempt amount reduce the supplement by **one dollar for every two dollars** over. The **2026 annual exempt amount is $24,480**, which is $2,040 per month. The reduction is applied in the year **after** the earnings were made, in administrative practice beginning with the **July** payment. For the year immediately following the first year of entitlement, the reduction cannot exceed the total supplement payable in that first year.

**Counted:** wages for services performed in the year plus net earnings from self employment, including bonuses and overtime, and including pay for work not covered by Social Security.
**Not counted:** the FERS annuity itself, TSP withdrawals, Social Security benefits, interest, dividends, capital gains, most rental income, inheritances, insurance proceeds.
**Exempt:** special provision retirees are exempt until they reach their MRA, after which the test applies.

Source, exempt amount: SSA, https://www.ssa.gov/oact/cola/rtea.html
Source, mechanics: CSRS/FERS Handbook Chapter 51, https://www.opm.gov/retirement-center/publications-forms/csrsfers-handbook/c051.pdf
Corroboration on the July timing: https://www.fedweek.com/experts-view/earnings-limits-for-fers-supplement-social-security/

**Legislative status, September 2026.** Elimination of the supplement for individuals not already entitled before January 2028 was proposed in H.R. 1 in the 119th Congress and passed the House in May 2025. It was **removed before enactment** when the Senate parliamentarian ruled the federal workforce provisions out of order under the Byrd rule. A proposed change from high-3 to high-5 and a proposed increase in employee contributions were also dropped. **No change to the FERS supplement or to the annuity computation is enacted as of September 2026.**

Source: https://www.govexec.com/pay-benefits/2025/06/all-provisions-targeting-federal-worker-benefits-unions-stricken-senate-reconciliation-package/406395/
Source: https://www.narfe.org/blog/2025/07/01/federal-workforce-provisions-dropped-from-h-r-1-prior-to-senate-passage/
Source: https://www.fedsmith.com/2025/07/09/federal-employee-proposals-scrapped-from-one-big-beautiful-bill/

**Source conflict, flagged and important.** At least one widely read outlet published an issue brief describing these provisions as enacted. That article describes the House passed version of May 2025, not the final law. Rely on the GovExec, NARFE and FedSmith accounts.

**Forward looking caution.** These proposals were defeated on procedural grounds, not on the merits. They can return in standalone legislation. For a separation five to seven years out, model current law and run one sensitivity case with the supplement set to zero.

## 9. FERS COST OF LIVING ADJUSTMENTS

**The age 62 rule.** 5 U.S.C. 8462(c)(3) provides that an adjustment "shall not be effective with respect to the annuity of an annuitant who is under 62 years of age as of the date on which such adjustment would otherwise first take effect."

**Exceptions that receive a COLA before 62:** special provision retirees, being law enforcement officers, firefighters and air traffic controllers; FERS disability annuitants; military reserve technicians separated due to disability; and survivor annuitants.

Source: 5 U.S.C. 8462, https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/5/8462
Source: OPM COLA FAQ, https://www.opm.gov/support/retirement/faq/cost-of-living-adjustments/

**The diet COLA formula.** Let C be the CPI-W percent change, third quarter average year over year, rounded to the nearest tenth of one percent. That figure is the CSRS COLA.

| CPI-W increase | FERS COLA | CSRS COLA |
|---|---|---|
| Under 2.0 percent | Full C | C |
| 2.0 to 3.0 percent | 2.0 percent flat | C |
| Above 3.0 percent | C minus 1.0 percentage point | C |

Source: 5 U.S.C. 8462(b), https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/5/8462

**2026 figures**, announced October 24, 2025, effective December 1, 2025, first appearing in the January 2026 payment: **CSRS 2.8 percent, FERS 2.0 percent, Social Security 2.8 percent.** That is the diet COLA in operation, with CPI-W inside the 2 to 3 band capping FERS at 2 percent.

Source: OPM COLA FAQ, https://www.opm.gov/support/retirement/faq/cost-of-living-adjustments/
Source: https://www.narfe.org/blog/2025/10/24/annual-cola-falls-short-for-fers-retirees/

**Not verified.** No primary or credible secondary source states a single historical average FERS COLA figure. If a model needs one, label it `[ESTIMATE]` and state the derivation.

**Proposed, not enacted.** The Equal COLA Act, H.R. 491 and S. 624 in the 119th Congress, would give FERS annuitants the same COLA as CSRS. It remains in committee.

Source: https://www.congress.gov/bill/119th-congress/house-bill/491

## 10. ANNUITY COMMENCEMENT AND THE SEPARATION DATE

**5 U.S.C. 8464:** an annuity "commences on the first day of the month after separation from the service" for employees retiring under 5 U.S.C. 8412, which covers voluntary and optional retirement.

Source: https://uscode.house.gov/view.xhtml?req=granuleid%3AUSC-prelim-title5-section8464&num=0&edition=prelim

**Practical consequence.** FERS annuities are not prorated within a month. Separating on the last day of a month starts the annuity on the first of the next month with no gap. Separating on the first or second of a month means working most of that month and still waiting until the following month's first day. Month end separation dates are the standard FERS choice for that reason. This differs from CSRS, where separation on the first, second or third of a month starts the annuity the next day.

## 11. VOLUNTARY EARLY RETIREMENT AUTHORITY

**Thresholds:** age 50 with 20 years of creditable service, or any age with 25 years. The position must be covered by the agency's approved authority and separation must occur within the approved window.

**Reduction:** OPM states verbatim, "There is no annuity reduction in FERS for employees who retire on an early voluntary retirement under age 55." A CSRS component in a transferee's annuity is reduced by 1/6 of 1 percent per full month under age 55.

Source: OPM VERA, https://www.opm.gov/policy-data-oversight/workforce-restructuring/voluntary-early-retirement-authority/

Discontinued Service Retirement, meaning involuntary separation by reduction in force, directed reassignment refusal, or abolishment of position, uses the same thresholds.

Source: OPM Types of Retirement, https://www.opm.gov/retirement-center/fers-information/types-of-retirement/

## 12. SURVIVOR ANNUITY

| Election | Survivor receives | Retiree's annuity reduced by |
|---|---|---|
| Full | 50 percent of the unreduced annuity | 10 percent |
| Partial | 25 percent of the unreduced annuity | 5 percent |
| None | Nothing | Nothing |

The survivor percentage applies to the unreduced basic annuity. **Spousal consent is required to elect less than the maximum**, including the partial election and no election. The combined total of all current and former spouse survivor annuities is capped at 50 percent of the self only annuity.

Source: OPM Survivor Benefits, https://www.opm.gov/retirement-center/survivor-benefits/
Source: OPM FERS Computation, https://www.opm.gov/retirement-center/fers-information/computation/
Source: CSRS/FERS Handbook Chapter 52, https://www.opm.gov/retirement-center/publications-forms/csrsfers-handbook/c052.pdf

**The FEHB consequence.** OPM: "A surviving spouse can continue health benefits coverage if a monthly survivor annuity is payable to the surviving spouse." With no survivor annuity payable, the spouse's FEHB ends at the retiree's death with only a one time conversion opportunity. **The 5 percent partial election is sufficient to preserve it.**

Source: OPM Survivor Benefits, https://www.opm.gov/retirement-center/survivor-benefits/

**Not verified.** The additional condition that the spouse must be covered under a Self Plus One or Self and Family enrollment at the time of death is stated in secondary sources but was not located on a primary OPM page. Confirm with the agency benefits officer.

## 13. ANNUAL LEAVE LUMP SUM

**Valuation.** The agency projects the leave forward as if the employee had remained on duty, applying hours to successive workdays and holidays. Any statutory pay adjustment effective during that projected period is paid at the higher rate.

**Pay elements included:** rate of basic pay, locality payments, special rate supplements, availability pay, night differential for Federal Wage System employees, AUO, supervisory differentials, regularly scheduled overtime for FWS employees, non foreign COLAs, post differentials and foreign area allowances. **Excluded:** retention incentives and allowances paid solely to retain an employee.

Source: OPM, Lump Sum Payments for Annual Leave, https://www.opm.gov/policy-data-oversight/pay-leave/leave-administration/fact-sheets/lump-sum-payments-for-annual-leave/

**Ceilings.** 240 hours for most employees, 360 hours overseas, 720 hours for SES, Senior Level and Scientific and Professional. An employee separating mid leave year is paid for leave above the ceiling that has not yet been forfeited, so a typical retiree can be paid the carried over balance plus the leave accrued during the final year.

Source: OPM Annual Leave Fact Sheet, https://www.opm.gov/policy-data-oversight/pay-leave/leave-administration/fact-sheets/annual-leave/

**Tax treatment.** Ordinary wage income in the year received. Federal and state income tax, Social Security and Medicare are withheld. Health and life insurance premiums are not withheld, and **the payment cannot be deferred into the TSP.** The OPM fact sheet does not address taxation; this rests on secondary sources.

Source: https://www.fedweek.com/retirement-financial-planning/whats-in-a-lump-sum-payment-of-unused-annual-leave/

**Not creditable.** Unused annual leave adds nothing to creditable service and nothing to the high-3.

## 14. FEHB IN RETIREMENT

**The five year rule.** Two conditions, both required: entitlement to an immediate annuity, and continuous FEHB enrollment for the five years of service immediately before the annuity starts, or for the full period since first eligible if less than five years.

**What counts:** enrollment in your own name; coverage as a family member under someone else's enrollment; TRICARE, TRICARE for Life or CHAMPVA coverage provided you are enrolled in FEHB on the date of retirement and re enrolled within 60 days of losing the military coverage. **Medicare does not count.**

**Failing it** forfeits the right to carry FEHB into retirement permanently, leaving a 31 day extension, conversion to an individual policy, and Temporary Continuation of Coverage for up to 18 months at 102 percent of the full premium.

**Waiver.** OPM holds waiver authority, but in practice it is pre approved only where the employee was covered continuously since the start of the agency's buyout or early out authority and takes a VSIP buyout, a VERA early out, or a discontinued service retirement in the face of involuntary separation.

Source: OPM FEHB Handbook, Annuitants, https://www.opm.gov/healthcare-insurance/healthcare/reference-materials/reference/annuitants/
Source: OPM waiver FAQ, https://www.opm.gov/frequently-asked-questions/insure-faq/health/how-would-i-get-a-waiver-of-the-5-year-coverage-requirement-to-continue-health-benefits-into-retirement/
Source: OPM TCC, https://www.opm.gov/healthcare-insurance/healthcare/temporary-continuation-of-coverage/

**Government share.** Identical in retirement. The formula pays the lesser of 72 percent of the program wide weighted average premium or 75 percent of the selected plan's total premium. Annuitants receive the same benefits and the same government contribution as active employees in the same plan.

Source: OPM Cost of Insurance, https://www.opm.gov/healthcare-insurance/healthcare/reference-materials/reference/cost-of-insurance/

**Premium conversion.** 5 CFR 892.202 states that only current employees enrolled in FEHB are covered by the premium conversion plan and that former employees are not eligible. **Annuitant premiums are withheld from the annuity on a post tax basis.** The same applies to FEDVIP.

Source: 5 CFR Part 892, https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-5/chapter-I/subchapter-B/part-892

**Cancel versus suspend.** An annuitant who cancels FEHB can never re enroll. An annuitant may **suspend** coverage for Medicare Advantage, TRICARE, CHAMPVA, Medicaid or Peace Corps service and re enroll during a later Open Season or within the window around an involuntary loss of that coverage.

Source: OPM Form RI 79-9, https://www.opm.gov/forms/pdf_fill/ri79-9.pdf

## 15. FEHB AND MEDICARE

**Part B is optional for an FEHB annuitant.** OPM: "For most people covered by the FEHB Program, Medicare Part B is optional." FEHB enrollment and premium are unaffected.

**Who pays first.** Once you are an annuitant and Medicare enrolled, Medicare pays first and FEHB is secondary. While you are an active employee, FEHB pays first.

Source: OPM, Understand which insurance pays first, https://www.opm.gov/healthcare-insurance/medicare/understand-which-insurance-pays-first/
Source: OPM FastFacts, FEHB and Medicare, https://www.opm.gov/healthcare-insurance/fastfacts/fehbmedicare.pdf

**Cost of declining Part B.** A lifetime late enrollment penalty of 10 percent of the standard premium for each full 12 month period of delay, added permanently. Retiree coverage does not create a special enrollment period, so the 8 month special enrollment period based on current employment does not apply. Also forfeited: plan features that waive deductibles and coinsurance when Medicare is primary, eligibility for FEHB sponsored Medicare Advantage options and their Part B premium reimbursements, and coverage for out of network physicians that Part B would cover.

Source: https://www.medicare.gov/basics/costs/medicare-costs/avoid-penalties
Source: https://www.medicare.gov/basics/get-started-with-medicare/medicare-basics/working-past-65
Source: OPM Medicare, https://www.opm.gov/healthcare-insurance/healthcare/medicare/

**2026 Medicare figures.** Standard Part B premium $202.90 per month. Part B annual deductible $283.

Source: CMS, https://www.cms.gov/newsroom/fact-sheets/2026-medicare-parts-b-premiums-deductibles

**2026 Part B IRMAA.** Thresholds are modified adjusted gross income, and the total monthly premium is the standard amount plus the adjustment.

| Single MAGI | Married filing jointly MAGI | Total Part B premium |
|---|---|---|
| Up to $109,000 | Up to $218,000 | $202.90 |
| $109,001 to $137,000 | $218,001 to $274,000 | $284.10 |

## 16. FEDVIP IN RETIREMENT

**No five year rule.** OPM answers flatly: "No, there is no five-year rule with FEDVIP." Coverage continues into retirement with no minimum enrollment period, and an annuitant may enroll for the first time in retirement during Open Season or a qualifying life event.

**Not eligible:** deferred annuitants. An MRA+10 postponed annuitant loses FEDVIP at separation but may enroll again when the postponed annuity begins.

## 17. FEGLI IN RETIREMENT

**The five year rule.** Four conditions, all required: entitlement to an immediate annuity; insured for the five years of service immediately before the annuity starts, or for the full period eligible if less; enrolled on the date of retirement; and not converted to an individual policy. **The test is applied separately to Basic and to each Option.**

**No waiver.** OPM: "OPM has no authority to waive the requirements for continuing life insurance coverage."

Source: OPM FAQ, https://www.opm.gov/frequently-asked-questions/insure-faq/life/what-is-the-five-yearall-opportunity-rule-for-continuing-life-insurance-into-retirement/

| 50 percent reduction | Reduces 1 percent per month | Floors at 50 percent of BIA |

## 18. TSP

**The age 55 rule.** The 10 percent additional tax does not apply to payments made after you separate from service **during or after the calendar year you reach age 55**. It is keyed to the calendar year of separation, not the date.

**The age 50 rule for special category employees.** For a qualified public safety employee, meaning federal law enforcement officers, Customs and Border Protection officers, federal firefighters and air traffic controllers, the exception applies after separation during or after the year of reaching age 50, **or after 25 years of service**, which removes the age floor entirely.

**Rolling the TSP to an IRA forfeits the exemption.** The IRS exceptions table marks both the age 55 separation exception and the age 50 public safety exception as applying to qualified plans only and not to IRAs. Once the money is in an IRA it is an IRA distribution and the 10 percent tax applies until 59 and a half unless a different exception is met.

Source: TSP, Tax Rules about TSP Payments, https://www.tsp.gov/publications/tspbk26.pdf
Source: IRS Topic 558, https://www.irs.gov/taxtopics/tc558
Source: IRS exceptions table, https://www.irs.gov/retirement-plans/plan-participant-employee/retirement-topics-tax-on-early-distributions

**Practical consequence for anyone retiring at 55 to 59.** Leave enough in the TSP to cover the bridge years before rolling anything to an IRA.

**Withdrawal options.** Partial distributions with a $1,000 minimum, total distributions, installment payments with a $25 minimum, and a life annuity purchase with a $3,500 minimum applied separately to the traditional and Roth balances. Installments may be monthly, quarterly or annual, and **the amount and frequency may be changed at any time**, a change effective September 15, 2019 that replaced the old once per year restriction. **An annuity purchase is irrevocable.**

Source: TSP, Withdrawals in Retirement, https://www.tsp.gov/withdrawals-in-retirement/
Source: TSP Distributions booklet, https://www.tsp.gov/publications/tspbk25.pdf

**2026 contribution limits.**

| Item | 2026 |
|---|---|
| Elective deferral limit, traditional and Roth combined | $24,500 |
| Age 50 catch up, ages 50 to 59 and 64 and over | $8,000 |
| Higher catch up, ages 60 to 63 | $11,250 |
| Annual additions limit, all sources | $72,000 |
| IRA contribution limit | $7,500 |
| IRA catch up, age 50 and over | $1,100 |

Source: TSP Bulletin 25-3, https://www.tsp.gov/bulletins/25-3/
Source: IRS, https://www.irs.gov/newsroom/401k-limit-increases-to-24500-for-2026-ira-limit-increases-to-7500
Source: IRS Notice 2025-67, https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-drop/n-25-67.pdf

**Roth catch up requirement for high earners.** From 2026, all catch up contributions must be designated Roth for anyone whose prior year FICA wages from the sponsoring employer exceeded the threshold, which is **$150,000 of 2025 wages** for 2026 contributions. The TSP applies it automatically.

Source: IRS Notice 2023-62, https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-drop/n-23-62.pdf
Source: TSP SECURE 2.0, https://www.tsp.gov/news-and-resources/secure-2-0-and-the-tsp/

**Roth TSP and required minimum distributions.** Under current law the Roth TSP balance is **not** subject to lifetime required minimum distributions, effective tax year 2024, and Roth distributions do not count toward satisfying the traditional balance RMD. Beneficiaries remain subject to post death distribution rules.

Source: TSP SECURE 2.0, https://www.tsp.gov/news-and-resources/secure-2-0-and-the-tsp/
Source: IRS RMD FAQs, https://www.irs.gov/retirement-plans/retirement-plan-and-ira-required-minimum-distributions-faqs

## 19. REQUIRED MINIMUM DISTRIBUTIONS

| Born | Applicable age |
|---|---|
| Before July 1, 1949 | 70 and a half |
| July 1, 1949 to December 31, 1950 | 72 |
| January 1, 1951 to December 31, 1958 | 73 |
| **January 1, 1960 or later** | **75** |

Source: 26 CFR 1.401(a)(9)-2, https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-26/section-1.401(a)(9)-2
Corroboration: TSP, "The RMD age increased to 73 on January 1, 2023 and will increase to 75 on January 1, 2033," https://www.tsp.gov/news-and-resources/secure-2-0-and-the-tsp/

**Birth year 1959 is unsettled.** The regulation leaves that subparagraph reserved, a residue of a SECURE 2.0 drafting conflict that assigned the cohort both 73 and 75. Anyone born in 1959 should treat this as an open item and confirm before relying on either.

**Required beginning date.** For an employer plan such as the TSP, April 1 of the calendar year following the later of the year the participant reaches the applicable age or the year the participant retires. For an IRA there is no still working exception. Each subsequent distribution is due by December 31, so taking the first one in the April grace window puts two distributions in the same tax year.

**Caution on IRS web pages.** Several IRS pages have not been updated for the age 75 tier and still state 73 or 72. The regulation is the controlling text.

## 20. SOCIAL SECURITY

**Full retirement age is 67** for everyone born in 1960 or later.

**Claiming at 62 with a full retirement age of 67:** the worker benefit is reduced 30 percent, the spousal benefit 35 percent. The mechanics are 5/9 of one percent per month for the first 36 months and 5/12 of one percent per additional month for the worker.

**Delayed retirement credits:** 8.0 percent per year for anyone born 1943 or later, accruing monthly and stopping at 70. A full retirement age of 67 delayed to 70 produces a 24 percent increase.

Source: https://www.ssa.gov/benefits/retirement/planner/agereduction.html
Source: https://www.ssa.gov/benefits/retirement/planner/delayret.html

**The statement projection assumption, confirmed.** SSA projects future earnings "by assuming that the worker continues to earn the same amount as in the year prior to the year of Statement receipt," continuing to the claiming age shown.

Source: SSA briefing paper BP2020-01, https://www.ssa.gov/policy/docs/briefing-papers/bp2020-01.html

**How to produce a zero future earnings estimate.** Sign in at https://www.ssa.gov/myaccount, open the Retirement Calculator, select a retirement age or date, and set the expected average future annual earnings field to **0**. The SSA Online Calculator at https://www.ssa.gov/oact/anypia/ also accepts future income but requires manual entry of the earnings history. The Quick Calculator is not suitable, because it adjusts past earnings rather than future earnings.

Source: https://www.ssa.gov/prepare/get-benefits-estimate
Source: https://www.ssa.gov/myaccount/assets/materials/EN-05-10686.pdf

**Taxation of benefits.** Provisional income equals adjusted gross income plus tax exempt interest plus half of Social Security benefits.

| Filing status | Up to 50 percent taxable above | Up to 85 percent taxable above |
|---|---|---|
| Single, head of household, qualifying surviving spouse | $25,000 | $34,000 |
| Married filing jointly | $32,000 | $44,000 |
| Married filing separately, lived together any part of the year | $0 | Taxable from the first dollar |

**These thresholds are not indexed to inflation.** The $25,000 and $32,000 thresholds come from the 1983 amendments and the $34,000 and $44,000 thresholds from 1993. SSA states that they "have remained unchanged since Congress first established them", by design, so the share of beneficiaries owing tax on benefits rises every year.

Source: SSA policy paper IP2015-02, https://www.ssa.gov/policy/docs/issuepapers/ip2015-02.html
Source: IRS, https://www.irs.gov/faqs/social-security-income

**For a FERS retiree this is structural.** The FERS annuity is fully taxable and counts in adjusted gross income, so most FERS retirees clear the 85 percent threshold on the annuity alone.

## 21. THE SOCIAL SECURITY FAIRNESS ACT

The Windfall Elimination Provision and the Government Pension Offset were **fully repealed** by P.L. 118-273, signed **January 5, 2025**, applying to monthly benefits payable after December 2023.

Source: SSA, https://www.ssa.gov/benefits/retirement/social-security-fairness-act.html
Source: CRS R49058, https://www.everycrsreport.com/reports/R49058.html

**FERS employees were never subject to either provision.** FERS is a covered system and FERS employees pay Social Security payroll taxes on all federal service. WEP and GPO applied to CSRS and to non covered state and local systems. A career FERS retiree gained nothing from the repeal because nothing was being withheld.

**The exception** is an employee who transferred from CSRS and retains a CSRS component in the annuity, or who has non covered service from outside federal employment. Ask about non covered employment for exactly that reason.

## 22. THE SENIOR DEDUCTION

Enacted in July 2025. In current law for tax years **2025 through 2028**, expiring after 2028.

| Element | Rule |
|---|---|
| Amount | $6,000 per qualifying individual, $12,000 where both spouses are 65 or older |
| Age | Must attain age 65 on or before the last day of the taxable year |
| Availability | Both itemizers and non itemizers, in addition to the existing extra standard deduction for those 65 and over |
| Phase out begins | $75,000 modified adjusted gross income single, $150,000 joint |
| Phase out rate | 6 percent of MAGI above the threshold |
| Fully eliminated at | $175,000 single, $250,000 joint |
| Filing | Married taxpayers must file jointly to claim it |

Source: IRS, https://www.irs.gov/newsroom/check-your-eligibility-for-the-new-enhanced-deduction-for-seniors
Source: IRS, https://www.irs.gov/newsroom/one-big-beautiful-bill-act-tax-deductions-for-working-americans-and-seniors

**It does not exempt Social Security benefits from tax** and does not change the provisional income thresholds in section 20. It is a separate deduction against taxable income.

The phase out rate and the full elimination points are not stated on IRS pages and come from secondary tax analysis. Verify before relying on them.
